CapFlow

Move YouTube captions anywhere on the screen

YouTube puts captions at the bottom of the video, so your eyes keep jumping between the picture and the text. CapFlow is a Chrome extension that moves the captions into a floating liquid-glass line you can drag anywhere on the video: next to the speaker, near the action, wherever you're already looking.

  • Drag the caption line anywhere on the video, or set its position with a slider.
  • Words appear one at a time as they're spoken, and a new line starts when the speaker changes.
  • Set the text size, and how frosted or clear the glass is.
  • Works with YouTube's own captions, including auto-generated ones, in fullscreen and theatre mode.

Caption placement FAQ

Common questions about where captions sit on a YouTube video, and how to move them.

Moving captions on the screen

Can you change where captions appear on the screen on YouTube?

Only a little. YouTube's caption options let you change the font, color, size and background, but there's no setting for position: captions sit near the bottom of the video. On a computer you can drag the captions to another spot with your mouse, but YouTube doesn't keep that as a setting.

CapFlow adds the missing control. It shows the captions as one floating glass line that you place anywhere on the video, by dragging it in a preview or with a position slider, and it stays where you put it for every video.

How do I move captions on YouTube?

On youtube.com in a computer browser, click and hold the captions while the video plays and drag them where you want. That only lasts for what you're watching; it isn't a saved setting.

For a position that sticks, install CapFlow: open its toolbar popup, drag the caption in the preview or use the position slider, and every video uses that spot.

How do I move captions to the top of my YouTube video?

YouTube has no “captions on top” option. On a computer you can drag the captions up to the top of the player by hand for the video you're watching.

CapFlow puts its caption line at the top right of the video by default, clear of the bottom of the frame where on-screen text, scores and name titles usually sit. You can move it anywhere else from its popup.

Why are YouTube subtitles in the middle of the screen?

Some caption tracks carry their own position. Broadcast-style captions, common on YouTube TV and on uploads of TV shows, and some uploaded subtitle files tell the player where to draw each line, so they can land mid-screen over faces or the action. YouTube doesn't let viewers override that.

On youtube.com videos, CapFlow ignores where the track wants the text and draws its caption line wherever you've put it. Turn YouTube's own CC off and only CapFlow's line shows.

Can I move captions in the YouTube app on iPhone or Android?

The YouTube app has no caption position setting on iPhone or Android. You can change caption size and style, but not where they sit.

CapFlow is a Chrome extension for computers, so it doesn't run in the phone app. If you watch on a laptop or desktop, it lets you place captions anywhere on the video.

Can I move closed captions on YouTube TV?

YouTube TV has no caption position setting. Live TV captions come from the broadcaster with their own placement, which is why they sometimes sit in the middle or at the top of the screen.

CapFlow runs on youtube.com, not YouTube TV, so it can't change those. For regular YouTube videos on a computer, it lets you choose exactly where captions go.

Caption size, color and style

Where are YouTube's caption settings?

On a computer, start a video, click the Settings gear in the player, choose Subtitles/CC, then Options. There you can change the font, font color and size, background and window color and opacity, and the character edge style. Account-wide options, like always showing captions, are in your YouTube settings under Playback and performance.

Position isn't one of them. That's what CapFlow adds, along with its own text size and a glass background that runs from frosted to clear.

How do I change caption size on YouTube on a PC?

Click the Settings gear in the player, choose Subtitles/CC → Options, and change Font size (50% to 400%). You can also press + and - while captions are on.

With CapFlow, the text size slider in its popup sets the size of its caption line, with a live preview, and you can move the line off the bottom of the video too.

How do I change caption size on YouTube mobile?

On Android, open the YouTube app's Settings and tap Captions; it opens Android's caption preferences, where you set text size and style. On iPhone, caption style comes from iOS: Settings → Accessibility → Subtitles & Captioning → Style.

On a computer, CapFlow gives you a size slider and also lets you move the captions anywhere on the video.

How do I change the color of captions on YouTube?

In the player, click Settings → Subtitles/CC → Options and change Font color, Background color and Window color. YouTube offers white, yellow, green, cyan, blue, magenta, red and black.

CapFlow takes a different approach: white text with a dark halo on a pane of liquid glass, so it stays readable on bright and dark scenes without a solid black box covering the picture.

Turning captions on and off

Why do I have captions on my YouTube, and why do they keep turning on?

Usually because Always show captions is on in your YouTube settings (Playback and performance), or because your phone or TV has captions turned on in its accessibility settings, which YouTube follows. Turn off whichever is on, then press the CC button once on a video.

If you want captions but find them in the way at the bottom of the screen, CapFlow lets you move them somewhere that works for you.

How do I turn off auto captions in the YouTube app?

While a video plays, tap the player, then tap CC (or open the gear or ⋮ menu → Captions → Turn off captions). If they come back on every video, turn captions off in the app's Settings → Captions on Android, or in iOS Settings → Accessibility → Subtitles & Captioning on iPhone, and turn off Always show captions in your YouTube playback settings.

On a computer, CapFlow's caption line has its own on/off switch in the toolbar popup, separate from YouTube's CC.

How do I turn off Live Caption on YouTube?

Live Caption isn't a YouTube feature. It's a captioning tool built into Android and Chrome that captions any audio playing on your device. On Android, press a volume button and tap the Live Caption icon under the volume slider. In Chrome, go to Settings → Accessibility and switch off Live Caption. YouTube's own captions are controlled separately with the CC button.

If you'd rather keep YouTube's captions and just put them somewhere better, CapFlow shows them in a floating line you can place anywhere on the video.

How do I turn on subtitles on YouTube without the CC button?

On a computer, press C, or open the Settings gear → Subtitles/CC and pick a track. In the app, tap the gear or ⋮ menu → Captions. If no tracks are listed, the video has no captions to show.

When a video has captions, CapFlow shows them in its glass line on its own, even with YouTube's CC switched off.

How do I force subtitles to appear on YouTube?

In your YouTube settings, open Playback and performance, turn on Always show captions and tick Include auto-generated captions (when available). Every video with captions then starts with them on.

CapFlow works the same way for its own line: once it's switched on, it shows captions on every video that has them, without pressing CC.

How do I get English subtitles on YouTube if they're not available?

If the video has captions in another language, open Settings → Subtitles/CC, pick that track, then choose Auto-translate → English. If the video has no captions at all, there's nothing to translate.

CapFlow picks the track in your language when the video has one, and prefers captions a person wrote over auto-generated ones.

How do I turn subtitles on or off on YouTube on a smart TV?

Play a video, press select or up on the remote to bring up the player controls, and choose the CC button. On some TVs it's under the gear or ⋮ menu instead. The same button turns them off.

Watching on a computer instead? CapFlow lets you move YouTube's captions anywhere on the video and restyle them.

When captions are missing or broken

Why are captions not available on some YouTube videos?

Captions only exist if the creator added them or YouTube generated them. Auto-captions can be missing when a video is brand new, has poor audio, music or lots of overlapping speech, is in a language YouTube can't auto-caption, or when the creator turned them off. Live streams often have no caption file to read.

CapFlow can only show captions YouTube has. When a video has none, its popup tells you so instead of leaving you guessing.

How do I fix YouTube captions not appearing or disappearing?

Common causes: CC was switched off, the next video has no captions, an ad interrupted playback, the app or browser needs an update, or an extension is interfering. Reload the page, press C, check Settings → Subtitles/CC for a track, and try a private window with extensions off. On Android, clearing the YouTube app's cache often helps.

CapFlow retries for you when YouTube's caption download fails, up to five times, and its popup shows what's happening (“Trying again (2 of 5)…”).

What happened to community captions on YouTube?

YouTube removed community contributions, the feature that let viewers write captions and translations for other people's videos, in September 2020, saying few people used it and it attracted spam. Creators can still add captions themselves or with help from people they trust.

CapFlow works with whatever captions a video has, including YouTube's auto-generated ones.

For creators

Where should captions go on Reels and Shorts?

Keep them in the middle band of the frame. On Reels, Shorts and TikTok the bottom of the screen is covered by the description, buttons and username, and the right edge by the like and share buttons, so captions placed low get hidden. Many creators put them just below the center, near the speaker's face.

The same idea, captions near where your eyes already are, is what CapFlow brings to regular YouTube videos for viewers.

How do I edit captions on YouTube, including auto-generated ones?

Captions are edited in YouTube Studio: open Subtitles, pick the video and the language, then edit the text and timing. For auto-generated captions, choose Duplicate and edit to make an editable copy. Studio's subtitle editor is built for a computer; on a phone, open studio.youtube.com in your browser with the desktop site turned on.

Viewers with CapFlow see your captions word by word in a glass line they can move, so accurate timing pays off.

Why are YouTube's auto-generated captions in the wrong language?

Auto-captions are made in the language set for the video. If that's wrong, the captions come out as nonsense. Creators can fix it in YouTube Studio on the video's Details page under Video language. Viewers can't change it, but can use Auto-translate when a correct track exists.

CapFlow picks the track in the viewer's language when the video has one.
